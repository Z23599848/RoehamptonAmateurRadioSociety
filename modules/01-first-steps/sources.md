## Foundation syllabus mapping

The identifiers below refer specifically to the **Foundation column**. “Partial” means the learning point also contains material deferred to later weeks.

| Point | Week 1 treatment | Follow-up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1A1 | Partial: purpose and non-commercial use | Geographical and airborne/ship conditions |
| 1A2 | Partial: licence levels | Locators, suffixes and associated restrictions |

| 1D1 | Partial: interference responsibility | Inspection, restrictions and closedown |
| 1H1 | Practise status and two table rows | Further rows, restrictions and table exercises |
| 2E2 | Partial: frequency ranges and wave vocabulary | AC/AF/RF details and other specified frequencies |

The optional 300/f calculation supports intuition. The Foundation 2E7 column does not specify the formula-manipulation requirement found in the Intermediate column. This module is not a complete syllabus coverage claim.

## Changes made when developing the lesson

- Replaced the spoken-video format and promotions with learning outcomes, a classroom sequence and written activities.
- Corrected transcription errors such as “Anor/amer” to amateur, “MOS” to Morse and “mahz” to MHz.
- Corrected “millions of hertz per second”: MHz means millions of cycles per second.
- Distinguished time-axis frequency/period explanations from distance-axis wavelength diagrams.
- Treated wavelength band names as approximate. At 145 MHz the wavelength is about 2.07 m; at 14.2 MHz it is about 21.1 m. Exactly 20 m gives about 15 MHz, not an amateur 20-metre operating frequency.
- Used the current EX307 contents for exercises. The January 2026 booklet has the Foundation parameter table, specimen band plans and conversion chart; the lesson does not promise the separate general VHF allocation table mentioned in the transcript.
- Replaced unsupported or nonessential historical claims and operator-number rankings with practical beginner examples. These are not needed for this module's outcomes.
- Clarified that FT8 uses structured data exchanges and SSTV typically sends still pictures.
- Kept formula manipulation as an optional aid and identified incomplete syllabus coverage.
