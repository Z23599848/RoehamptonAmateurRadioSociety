## Worksheet answers

### Activity A - Identify the change

1. `MW7ABC`, as a classroom illustration only.
2. Use the Scotland RSL M if continuing to include an RSL: `MM7ABC`.
3. No. It is optional for an ordinary Foundation call sign, but must be accurate if used.
4. A 2-series Intermediate call sign must include the appropriate RSL.
5. No. A suffix changes neither power nor frequency permission.

Notify Ofcom immediately in writing of the recorded-address change. Periodic revalidation and reporting an actual change are separate responsibilities.

### Activity B - Whose responsibility?

- The unlicensed learner uses the supervisor's call sign and conditions, with permission and direct physical supervision.
- If that supervision is no longer present, stop the unlicensed learner's transmitting. Telephone availability does not replace the required arrangement.
- An independently operating Foundation visitor remains within Foundation conditions and uses their own assigned call sign, even on borrowed Full-level equipment.
- When the supervising licensee changes, identify using the new supervisor's call sign and apply that supervisor's conditions. Complete the handover before continuing.

### Activity C - Purpose, message and place

1. An initial CQ call is allowed to seek a contact, provided the station is properly identified and all other conditions are met.
2. Commercial advertising is outside the amateur licence's purpose.
3. Morse is a known code, not inherently encryption. The claim that every coded message is forbidden is wrong. Normal amateur encryption intended to hide meaning is restricted, with specific exceptions explained in the guide.
4. A UK Foundation licence does not by itself give authority to transmit from France. Check the foreign authority's arrangements before operating.
5. Captain's permission does not remove airborne restrictions: use only permitted primary allocations, the stipulated 500 mW EIRP limit and the allowed geographical context.

### Activity D - London table challenge

| Proposed frequency | Row | Status | Result of the lookup |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 145.500 MHz | 144-146 MHz | Primary | 25 W maximum PEP for ordinary terrestrial operation |
| 2401 MHz | 2400-2450 MHz | Secondary | 2 W maximum PEP; accept interference from ISM users |
| 431.500 MHz | 431-432 MHz | Secondary | Not available within the specified 100 km London radius |

A geographical exclusion is not merely a power cap. Lowering the power does not make the third example available. A valid frequency-row lookup also does not settle every other operating requirement.

The Ofcom-authorised logging instruction must be followed. The fact that a voluntary hobby log is otherwise optional is not a reason to refuse it.

## Focused follow-up

- If RSLs were confusing, explain the difference between the station's present location and the operator's home address.
- If supervision was confusing, rehearse a handover without a working transmitter: say whose call sign and limits apply at each stage.
- If the table was confusing, read the restriction column aloud before reading the power column.
- If identification was confusing, explain why clear identification is an ongoing responsibility, not a single fixed timer.
