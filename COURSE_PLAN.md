# RARS Foundation course: proposed weekly plan

**Audience:** complete beginners. **Format:** eight weekly meetings, with short reading and practice between sessions. Start with 90 minutes per meeting and extend or split later sessions if learners need more practice. This is a proposed teaching sequence, not an RSGB-prescribed course length.

| Week | Theme | What learners will practise | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Your first steps in amateur radio | Explain the hobby; classify HF/VHF/UHF; compare frequency and wavelength; recognise modes; read a licence-table row | Teaching draft complete |
| 2 | Your licence and responsible operation | Call signs, identification, permitted messages, supervision, licence maintenance, geographical limits and reference-table use | Planned |
| 3 | Electricity and radio building blocks | Units, conductors, current, voltage, resistance, power, simple circuits, component symbols and AC/DC | Planned |
| 4 | From microphone to radio signal | Transmitter/receiver blocks, modulation, tuning, filtering, microphone settings and clean transmissions | Planned |
| 5 | Antennas, feeders and propagation | Choose an antenna; recognise feeder/connectors; explain matching, SWR, polarisation and how signals travel | Planned |
| 6 | Interference and station safety | Diagnose simple EMC problems; manage electricity, batteries, antennas, outdoor risks and RF exposure | Planned |
| 7 | Making contacts | Listening, a first contact, phonetic alphabet, reports, CQ, nets, repeaters, band plans and operating courtesy | Planned |
| 8 | Revision and exam preparation | Practise the official reference booklet, revisit weak topics, take a full-syllabus mock and plan the licence application | Planned |

Safety and lawful operation should be reinforced throughout. Start with receive-only demonstrations and role-play; any live transmitting activity needs a suitable licensed supervisor and a station prepared for that activity.

## A consistent format for every week

1. State observable learning outcomes.
2. Introduce a useful situation or question.
3. Teach short explanations with original examples and diagrams.
4. Let learners apply the idea in a classroom activity.
5. Check understanding with original questions and explain the answers.
6. Set 20–30 minutes of focused homework.
7. Record the syllabus points covered, points only introduced, and remaining gaps.

## Preparing the later modules

Use the next transcript to identify topics, then author the lesson around the current Foundation learning points. Keep source acknowledgements and corrections in each module's `sources.md`. Distinguish background enrichment from examinable content, and mark partial coverage explicitly.

Before describing the whole course as exam-ready, map every item in the **Foundation column** of the current combined syllabus to teaching and assessment. The broad themes above are not a completed coverage audit. Combined-syllabus columns for Intermediate and Full must not be mistaken for Foundation requirements.

The [RSGB examination overview](https://rsgb.org/main/clubs-training/for-trainers/syllabus-2019/) currently lists 26 Foundation questions in 60 minutes, with 19 correct answers required. A short weekly quiz is a learning check, not a predictor or substitute for that examination.
